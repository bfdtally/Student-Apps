EVIDENCE A • ROOM 214

The hallway after the bell

The science trophy was supposed to be locked inside Room 214 before the final bell. When Maya arrived after school, the display case was empty. A trail of silver paper stars led from the case toward the library. Near the doorway, Maya noticed a note that said, “Look where stories sleep.”

Investigator tip: Look at the words around a clue. Supporting details can help you infer what the author means.

Worked example

Sentence: “The classroom was freezing, so Jordan zipped his coat all the way up.”

Think: The detail about zipping a coat gives a clue that freezing means very cold.

ABOUT SCHOLAR SLEUTH

Reading can be an investigation.

Scholar Sleuth is a focused course prototype for grades 6–8. It turns reading skills into a mystery challenge so learners practice finding evidence, making inferences, and explaining their thinking.

How it works

  1. Read the evidence.
  2. Use the worked example to see one way to think.
  3. Choose the answer that best fits the text.
  4. Use feedback to rethink an incorrect answer.
  5. Track your progress and revisit the toolkit.
HELP & ACCESSIBILITY

Built to support different readers.

  • Visible text stays on screen with spoken directions.
  • Buttons have clear labels and large touch targets.
  • Keyboard focus is visible.
  • The layout adapts to phone, tablet, and desktop widths.
  • Speech uses the browser’s built-in SpeechSynthesis API when available; it never replaces the written content.
